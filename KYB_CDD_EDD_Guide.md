# Know Your Business (KYB), Customer Due Diligence (CDD), and Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

**Simulated training only — no real entities, accounts, sanctions checks, or bank records.**

## Key distinctions

- **KYC — Know Your Customer:** Broad customer-identification and risk-understanding processes; applies to persons and businesses.
- **KYB — Know Your Business:** Business-customer verification and understanding. Examples: legal existence, registration details, operating address, ownership/control structure, business purpose, expected activity, and risk indicators.
- **CDD — Customer Due Diligence:** Understanding customer identity, nature/purpose of relationship and risk profile, and conducting appropriate ongoing monitoring. Business CDD often includes examining ownership and control information.
- **EDD — Enhanced Due Diligence:** Additional investigation or measures when the institution's risk-based policies or applicable requirements call for enhanced scrutiny; EDD is not automatically required whenever an anomaly appears.

## EDD decision framework (illustrative)

1. Identify a documented **risk indicator** (e.g., unexplained complex ownership, inconsistent activity, higher-risk geographic exposure or PEP concern).
2. **Validate** the data and distinguish a true discrepancy from stale or missing documents.
3. Evaluate **context**, beneficial owners/controllers, business model, expected transaction flows, source-of-funds questions when relevant, and screening results through approved processes.
4. Determine whether **additional due diligence** is warranted by policy and applicable rules. Document rationale; do not label all high-value transactions suspicious.
5. Record the **decision**, evidence reviewed, unanswered questions, and recommended follow-up or escalation. No real regulatory report is submitted.

## Practice deliverable

Create a 1-page KYB case memo: entity profile, ownership diagram, documentation checklist, known facts, open questions, provisional risk indicators, and a clearly justified request for additional information. A second memo can compare ordinary CDD and an EDD-triggering scenario.
